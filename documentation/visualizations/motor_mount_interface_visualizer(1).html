What you are seeing
The motor does not fill the whole tube

The 38 mm motor slides in from the aft end. A short motor sits at the back of the long motor mount. The empty tube volume forward of the motor is normal when you design for longer future motors.

ForwardLeft side of the figure. Recovery volume and nose direction are forward.
AftRight side of the figure. Nozzle, retainer cap, and motor insertion direction are aft.
No motor blockThere is no glued stop inside the long tube. The aft thrust ring/retainer defines motor position.
Open frontForward motor-mount end remains open unless you use electronics-only deployment and intentionally isolate volumes.

Thrust-ring / retainer interface
1
Motor thrust ring bears forwardThe motor case pushes on its aft thrust ring. The thrust ring’s forward face bears on the fixed retainer base / aft motor-mount shoulder.
2
Retainer base loads the motor tubeThe fixed base is bonded/screwed to the 38 mm motor mount tube. This is the local structural interface, not the outer airframe wall.
3
Centering rings bridge outwardThe motor mount tube passes thrust into the centering rings, which spread the load into the larger 2.6 in fiberglass airframe.
4
Retainer cap captures rearward motionThe threaded cap keeps the motor from sliding out during ejection, coast, landing shock, or handling. The cap is not the primary forward thrust stop.
Model notes
This visualization is intentionally simplified: dimensions are scaled for clarity, colors are non-physical, and epoxy fillets are shown as idealized load-transfer bands. Use the retainer manufacturer’s installation instructions for real dimensions, clearances, and bonding requirements.
