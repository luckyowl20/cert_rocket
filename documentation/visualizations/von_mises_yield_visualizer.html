Data source
Wikipedia, “Von Mises yield criterion”. The geometry in this viewer follows the article’s principal-stress-coordinate description: the yield surface is a circular cylinder around the hydrostatic axis with radius √(2/3)σᵧ. This visualization is normalized by tensile yield strength σᵧ.
How to read the axes
σ₁, σ₂, σ₃The three principal stresses. A point in this 3D space is one principal stress state.
Hydrostatic axisThe centerline σ₁ = σ₂ = σ₃. Moving along it adds equal pressure/tension to all directions.
Deviatoric planeA plane perpendicular to the hydrostatic axis. The cylinder cuts this plane as a circle.
Yield tubeThe material reaches yield when the stress point reaches the tube surface: σᵥ = σᵧ.

Important caveat: Von Mises is primarily a ductile-metal yield model. It is useful for understanding equivalent stress, but it is not a full composite failure model for G10/fiberglass airframes.
